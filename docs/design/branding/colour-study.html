KYNEX · Instrument — colour study

Kynex is a control panel for an agent that works on its own: you spend most of the time reading (transcripts, diffs), and every so often you have to act (approve, answer, send). Control panels have solved this colour problem already, so the palette follows the same rules they do.
1 · The housing is almost colourlessNeutrals at OKLCH chroma ≤ 0.006, with a faint warm tint like Braun's off-white plastic. Round 2 looked "weird" because the whole UI sat on a mid-tone, yellow-green-tinted grey (L≈0.86, chroma 0.012): too dim to read on, too tinted to look neutral.
2 · Colour means state, never decorationIndicator colours follow IEC 60073, the standard for machine lamps: green normal, amber needs attention, red fault. Every colour on screen means something.
3 · Agent and user get opposite huesClaude's identity is warm (terracotta, h≈40°). The operator's colour, used for your controls, focus and selection, is its complement, so the chrome never competes with the agent. It also lets warm alerts stand out against cool controls.
4 · Contrast is set, not guessedBody text ≈ 13:1, not 21:1: pure black on white causes glare during long reading. Muted text ≥ 5:1. In dark mode accents drop chroma and gain lightness so they don't bloom (halation).
